# JLPT grammar section: the real format

Source: the official sample tests at jlpt.jp (`https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2012/pdf/N3G.pdf`,
`N3answer.pdf`, `N2answer.pdf`), read page by page. Re-check there if anything looks off.

## Question counts

| 大問 | N3 | N2 |
|---|---|---|
| 文法形式の判断 | 問題1, 13 questions | 問題7, 12 questions |
| 文の組み立て | 問題2, 5 questions | 問題8, 5 questions |
| 文章の文法 | 問題3, 1 passage × 5 blanks | 問題9, 1 passage × 5 blanks |

Every question has exactly 4 choices, numbered 1–4. One is correct.

## Instructions, word for word (N3)

- 問題1: つぎの文の（　　）に入れるのに最もよいものを、1・2・3・4から一つえらびなさい。
- 問題2: つぎの文の ★ に入る最もよいものを、1・2・3・4から一つえらびなさい。
- 問題3: つぎの文章を読んで、文章全体の内容を考えて、[19] から [23] の中に入る最もよいものを、1・2・3・4から一つえらびなさい。

N2 uses 次の and 選びなさい in kanji.

## 文法形式の判断: what the real questions look like

- **Dialogues:** 10 of 13 in the N3 sample, but only 3 of 12 in the N2 sample: `A「…」B「…（　　）…」`, often with a
  scene tag in parentheses: `（会社で）`, `（スーパーで）`, `（デパートでシャツを選びながら）`.
  Speakers are A/B, family (兄・弟・妹・母親・子ども), 学生/先生, or names (山田・田中).
- The blank can sit anywhere: mid-sentence, before a verb, at the very end of a line.
- **All 4 choices share one shape**, and the trap is a small difference:
  - one stem conjugated 4 ways: `買い / 買え / 買う / 買える` (before 〜そうもない)
  - particle stacks: `までは / までには / までも / までにも`
  - near-synonym patterns: `ために / ように / ことに / みたいに`, `らしいなら / らしいのに / ようなら / ようなのに`
  - keigo direction: `来られても / いらっしゃっても / うかがっても / 行かれても`
  - giving/receiving and causative: `みさせてください / みてもいいでしょうか / みさせてほしいのですが / みたらどうでしょう`
  - long modal endings that differ in two places: `話しかけないほうがよさそうだな / 話しかけなくてもよさそうだな / 話しかけないほうがいいそうだな / 話しかけなくてもいいそうだな`
  - noun + particle: `田中さんが / 田中さんで / 田中さんって / 田中さんでも`
- The right answer is decided by context (who is speaking to whom, what came before),
  not just by connection rules. Wrong choices are usually grammatical somewhere else.
- Sample answers 1–13 were 2 2 4 3 4 1 2 3 4 4 2 3 1: spread evenly, no pattern.

## 文の組み立て: what the real questions look like

- Layout: `before ＿＿ ＿＿ ★ ＿＿ after`. ★ was in slot 3 in all 5 samples (the sentence
  may wrap, but ★ is still the 3rd blank).
- Chunks are **not single words**. They mix lone particles (`が`, `の`, `に`) with phrases
  (`何をやっていた`, `練習に来ないで`, `閉まる時間`, `違うから`, `と思うことは`).
- Dialogues are used here too (A asks, B answers with the scrambled part).
- The target pattern is spread across chunks so you have to rebuild it (solved order, ★ marks
  the graded chunk):
  `あの美術館は曜日 によって／閉まる時間／★が／違うから 窓口で確認したほうがいいよ。`,
  `おいしい野菜を育てる ことが／どんなに／★大変な／ことか わかりました。`,
  `わたしは、子どもが したい／と思うことは／★やらせて／やりたい と思っている。`
- Choices are listed as the 4 chunks in scrambled order; the answer is the number of the
  chunk that lands on ★.

## 文章の文法: what the real questions look like

- One passage, introduced by a line in plain form: `下の文章は、3か月前に日本に来た留学生の
  ダニエルさんが、「電車通学をして気がついたこと」について書いた作文である。`
- The passage has a title and the writer's name, about 450–500 characters in です/ます
  style, 3 paragraphs: an observation, examples, then a question or reflection.
- Blanks are numbered boxes (`[19]`) and test **flow**, not only target patterns:
  - verb aspect/direction: `わかってくるはずです / わかっていくそうです / わかってきました / わかっていったようです`
  - connectives: `したがって / つまり / たとえば / それから`
  - demonstratives (こ・そ・あ・ど): `ある電車 / そこの電車 / こういう電車 / どちらかの電車`
  - question words in embedded questions: `答えなのか / なぜなのか / 理由なのか / だれなのか`
  - endings that fit the writer's stance: `なるのでしょうか / なったでしょう / なってしまうのです / なってしまいました`
- Choices are often two-part (`わかって　きました`) with a space between the parts.

## What this means when writing questions

- Use dialogues and scene tags for about 3 in 4 of 文法形式の判断 at N3, about 1 in 4 at N2.
- Build distractors from the same stem or the same family, as above, and make the context
  the thing that decides.
- In 文の組み立て, mix particle chunks with phrase chunks at N3 (rarely at N2; its sample has none); ★ is usually slot 3.
- In 文章の文法, every blank tests a studied pattern (the user limits scope on purpose), decided
  by flow as the real test is: connectives, こそあど, aspect and sentence endings.
- Answers are spread across 1–4 with no pattern, and the right one isn't the longest.

## Topics: as wide as the real exam

The real N2 draws its sentences and passages from all of life, not a few stock scenes. Our
tests kept falling back on restaurants, cafés and remote work. Spread topics the way the exam does:

- **Daily life and family:** moving house, neighbours, chores, shopping, child-raising, grandparents, pets.
- **School and study:** classes, exams, club activities, teachers, studying abroad, libraries.
- **Work, but varied:** factories, shops, hospitals, farms, offices, job hunting, retiring. Not always an IT team.
- **Community and public life:** town notices, festivals, volunteering, local government, elections, disaster drills.
- **Health and the body:** doctors, illness, sleep, exercise, ageing.
- **Nature, science, environment:** weather and typhoons, animals and plants, space, recycling, energy.
- **Culture and leisure:** books, films, music, art, sport, travel, crafts, traditional arts, hobbies.
- **Society and ideas:** technology and its effects, language and dialects, manners, money, generations, media.

Rules:

- In one test, use each area at least once, and no single setting (restaurant, café, office,
  remote work) more than twice across all questions.
- Every passage has its own area and topic. N2 passages are columns or essays in だ/である style,
  as in the sample. At N3, vary the kind: an essay, a column, a notice, a student's 作文, a letter.
- Pick the situation before writing the sentence. Vary who speaks (family, teacher and student,
  neighbours, doctor and patient, shop clerk and customer, not only colleagues) and the scene tag
  (`（病院で）`, `（駅で）`, `（市役所で）`, `（家で）`).

## Make it as hard as the real exam

These rules keep the tests at real-exam level:

- **Out-of-scope choices.** Every fill-blank question whose choices are different patterns has at least one choice from outside the test's
  scope (other weeks, N3 basics, common N2 patterns). Knowing this week's list must not be enough.
- **Answers stay in scope, with a couple of exceptions.** The user limits scope on purpose, so almost
  every right answer is a point in scope. Crossing off the outsider must not be enough: at least 2 wrong
  choices are other in-scope points or other forms of the answer's pattern.
- **A couple of outsider answers.** In 2 fill-blank questions per test (1 if the section has fewer
  than 8), the right answer is the outside choice and an in-scope point is the trap. Tag the question
  with that trap point's `grammarId`. Never say which questions these are.
- **No form giveaways.** At least 2 of the 3 wrong choices fit the sentence's grammar (connection,
  tense, voice). Only meaning or context rules them out. At most one choice may be wrong by form alone.
  Exception: one stem in 4 forms (`買い / 買え / 買う / 買える`, real item 35's 答えさせた… + あげく),
  where the form is the point being tested.
- **Keigo, giving/receiving, voice.** About 1 in 4 fill-blank questions puts keigo (いらっしゃる /
  伺う / いただく), 〜てもらう / くれる / あげる, passive or causative into the 4 choices.
  Put them in the forms of an in-scope pattern (`待っていただく / お待ちになる / 待たせていただく /
  待ってさしあげる` + よりほかない). Count them before sending.
- **Connective choices fit the sentence.** Each of a 接続詞 question's 4 choices fits the sentence
  type (no それとも to start a statement), so only the flow decides.
- **Clue away from the blank.** Sentences are 15–70 characters with N2 vocabulary. The clue that decides
  sits in another clause or the other speaker's line. At most 1 in 3 questions is settled by a set
  phrase (経験の有無を問わず, 人目もかまわず). A fixed partner of the answer in the stem counts as
  a set phrase too (とは限らない → からといって, 右に出る者はいない → にかけては, せめて, ものか).
- **Passage blanks test flow.** Every answer is an in-scope point, but vary what decides it. At most
  2 blanks per passage are bare connectives. The rest put the point in forms where the writer's stance
  (のだ / のではないだろうか / てしまう), てくる・ていく, voice or a こそあど reference decides.
- **Big 並べ替え chunks.** At N2, at most one chunk is a lone particle. At least two chunks are 2+ words.
  `before` usually doesn't end on the word the pattern attaches to.
- **Self-check.** Before using a question, ask: could someone who only knows this week's list, reading
  only the words next to the blank, get it right? If yes, rewrite it, unless it is one of the
  set-phrase questions allowed above (at most 1 in 3).

## Review test (総復習)

When the user asks for a review test (総復習, "review test", "mixed test"), cover every studied week
of the level, not one week, at the real exam's counts: N2 12 / 5 / 5 (one passage), N3 13 / 5 / 5.
Pick points evenly across the weeks, favouring ones missed before. Title: `総復習 第1〜8週`.
